# Bain Luck: The Probability Layer for Everything

## The Problem

The score is lying to you.

When Carlos Alcaraz beat Jannik Sinner in the 2025 French Open final, the scoreboard read 3-0 in sets. A blowout, right? Except it wasn't. The match featured multiple tiebreaks, dramatic momentum swings, and stretches where Sinner looked like the better player. The score said domination; the reality was a coin flip that happened to land heads three times in a row.

Or take Scottie Scheffler at the Masters. After three rounds, he might sit "only" two shots ahead on the leaderboard. Casual fans see a tight race. But his win probability is 85% or higher, because of how consistently he converts from that position. The score looks close; the probability tells you it's already over.

We live in a world drowning in scores, standings, and stats — but starved of the one thing that actually tells us what's happening: **probability**.

## The Gambling Problem

Here's the irony: the best probability information in the world already exists. Sportsbooks, prediction markets, and statistical models produce remarkably accurate forecasts across every domain — sports, politics, economics, weather, entertainment.

But try to access it.

Go to any sportsbook and you'll be hit with deposit bonuses, parlays, and "risk-free" first bets before you can find the information you came for. The odds are presented in formats designed for gamblers — American odds (-150/+130), decimal odds, fractional odds — not for regular people who just want to know what's likely to happen.

This isn't a minor inconvenience. It's a public health issue.

Sports gambling has exploded since the Supreme Court struck down the federal ban in 2018. Nearly 50 million Americans placed a sports bet last year. Problem gambling rates among young adults have doubled. Every major sports broadcast now features live betting integrations. Every stadium has a sportsbook lounge.

The information people want — "What are the chances my team wins tonight?" — has been locked behind an industry designed to take their money.

Bain Luck exists to separate probability from gambling.

## Why Probabilities Matter

People already think in probabilities. They just don't realize it.

- "There's maybe a 60% chance it rains — I'll grab an umbrella."
- "I think there's a decent shot Vrabel turns out to be a great coach."
- "The election feels like a toss-up to me."

These are probability judgments. We make dozens of them every day. The difference is that for many of these questions — Will it rain? Who wins the election? Will my team make the playoffs? — there are actual, well-calibrated probability estimates available. They're just buried in sportsbook lines, prediction market prices, and statistical models that most people never see.

What if all of that information was translated into plain language, aggregated into one place, and presented without a single gambling ad?

## What We Built

Bain Luck translates the world's probability sources into something anyone can understand.

Instead of "-150 / +130," you see **"60% vs 40%."** Instead of checking three different sportsbooks and two prediction markets, you get **one aggregated probability** drawn from six independent sources — each weighted by reliability and cross-validated against the others.

The product covers far more than sports:

- **Politics**: Presidential election odds, congressional races, Supreme Court decisions
- **Economics**: Fed rate decisions, recession probability, inflation forecasts
- **Entertainment**: Oscar predictions, box office performance, streaming viewership
- **Weather**: Temperature records, hurricane probability, seasonal outlooks
- **Sports**: Every major league, live win probability, player props, championship futures

Every probability comes with context. When a number moves, you see why. When sources disagree, you see the spread. When a market resolves, the outcome feeds back into our accuracy tracking.

### The Higher/Lower Game

Bain Luck isn't just a dashboard — it's a social experience.

The Discover feed presents a stream of probability questions: "Will the Fed cut rates in June?" "Will this movie hit $100M opening weekend?" "Will the Celtics repeat?"

For each card, you guess: **Higher or Lower** than the current market price. No money, no stakes — just your judgment against the crowd's. Your predictions are tracked, streaks are rewarded, and you can see how your intuition stacks up across categories.

It turns probability literacy into a game. People who play regularly start developing genuine forecasting intuition — noticing when their gut disagrees with the market, and learning when to trust which.

### Accuracy You Can Verify

On the calibration page, anyone can see exactly how accurate the data is. When Bain Luck says something has a 70% chance of happening, it happens roughly 70% of the time — across 151,000 resolved outcomes and counting.

This isn't a marketing claim. It's a live, public dashboard that updates automatically. The data is transparent, the methodology is open, and the results speak for themselves: our aggregate probabilities are accurate to within about 4 percentage points across all categories.

## How We're Different

**From sportsbooks and betting apps:**
Bain Luck has no betting, no deposits, no wagering of any kind. We present probability as information, not as an invitation to gamble. You'll never see a "Bet Now" button or a deposit bonus. The business model is fundamentally different from gambling.

**From news and media:**
Most coverage tells you what happened. Bain Luck tells you what's likely to happen, drawn from the best available evidence. When pundits say "this race is tight," we can show you whether the market agrees — and what "tight" actually means in percentage terms.

**From prediction market platforms (Kalshi, Polymarket):**
These platforms are sources, not competitors. We aggregate their data alongside sportsbook odds, ESPN models, statistical projections, and more. Individual platforms have coverage gaps and liquidity constraints; our aggregation fills those holes and produces a more reliable signal. And crucially, you don't need to create an account, deposit funds, or navigate a trading interface to see the information.

**From stat model sites (FiveThirtyEight, etc.):**
Statistical models are one input. We combine them with real-money market prices, which capture information that models miss — injury news, weather changes, lineup decisions, and the collective judgment of thousands of informed participants. The result is more accurate and more responsive than any single source.

## Why Now

Three forces are converging:

**1. The prediction market boom.** Kalshi and Polymarket have brought real-money forecasting to politics, economics, entertainment, and culture — domains that were sportsbook-only or model-only five years ago. For the first time, there are liquid, reliable probability sources for nearly everything people care about. But these platforms are built for traders, not casual consumers.

**2. The gambling backlash.** Public awareness of problem gambling is rising fast. States are reconsidering how aggressively betting apps can advertise. Parents are worried about their kids. There's a growing demand for the INFORMATION that odds provide without the INDUSTRY that exploits it.

**3. Probability literacy is rising.** COVID normalized probabilistic thinking for millions of people. Weather apps show percentage chances of rain. Election coverage uses probability models. People are more comfortable with "60% likely" than they were a decade ago — and more hungry for it.

Bain Luck sits at the intersection of all three: a consumer-friendly layer on top of the prediction market revolution, positioned on the right side of the gambling debate, arriving just as the audience is ready for it.
