READING IT — and the finding is that the DISCRIMINATOR ITSELF is not sound here.

Two intervals give **negative implied arrivals**, which is arithmetically impossible
under pure consumption: more messages left the queue than were executed plus
published. Taken at face value that is direct evidence of LOSS, and it is the
single strongest signal this window produced for #1996.

But it may not be taken at face value, for a reason this same file demonstrates:

  * `stats.total` is a PER-WORKER-PROCESS cumulative counter, summed here across
    whatever workers answered the broadcast. A worker that does not reply
    contributes ZERO, and the sum silently under-counts — which is
    indistinguishable from loss.
  * Two ticks returned NO replies at all (`totals: {}`, `active: null`), 34 minutes
    into a worker generation that `heroku ps` shows had been up since 05:44:21Z.
    So broadcast failure under load is not hypothetical here; it is observed, twice,
    in twelve samples.
  * Worse, the failures CLUSTER on the drops. The instrument that is supposed to
    discriminate loss becomes unreliable in exactly the conditions that produce the
    phenomenon it is measuring.

⚠️ AND MY OWN SAMPLER COMMITTED GOTCHA #53 WHILE MEASURING THIS. An empty inspect
was recorded as `exec = 0`, which read as a counter RESET — and a counter reset
reads as a worker restart. I very nearly reported "a restart coincides with a
113-message drop", which would have been support for candidate L1 manufactured
out of an absent observation. `heroku ps` refuted it: no restart. Empty totals are
now rendered UNKNOWN above, and the delta chain is BROKEN across them rather than
bridged — a delta computed across a gap is a claim about a period nobody watched.

VERDICT: LOSS leads, and is NOT established. #1996 stays open.

WHAT THE NEXT WINDOW SHOULD BUILD, since `stats.total` cannot answer this:
  1. Per-worker reply tracking, so a partial broadcast is VISIBLE as partial rather
     than arriving as a smaller number.
  2. Better: a Redis-side accounting that needs no broadcast at all — celery's
     `task_prerun` already writes a per-task delivery counter (`get_all_task_deliveries`),
     which is a worker-side WRITE rather than a control-plane READ, and so does not
     depend on a worker answering a question while it is busy.
  3. `INFO stats evicted_keys` sampled across a drop, to eliminate or confirm the
     Redis `allkeys-lru` candidate (L2) — the instance was at 41.45M when measured.
Route 2 is the cheap one and it exists already; it was simply never differenced.
