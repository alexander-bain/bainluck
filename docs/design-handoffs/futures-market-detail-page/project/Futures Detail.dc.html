The page every Discover tap lands on. Their market pages exist to place a trade; ours exists to make a probability legible. Every transactional surface is stripped — that space buys meaning, history, and the one thing no rival page can show: every source aggregated into a single number, with the disagreement one tap away.

1
Entity art, never stock
TMDB poster, team crest, or flag — whatever the market is about. Falls back to an initials chip, the same codepath the feed card uses. No photography, ever.
2
64px hero numeral — the loudest thing on the page
One step above the event-detail 36px token. JetBrains Mono, tabular, −0.045em. The % drops to 28px so the integer dominates. Identical type to the feed card they just tapped — continuity is the whole point.
3
Ambient history behind the number
The 7-day curve as texture — 1.5px line at 50% opacity, 7% area fill. It hints at the chart below without competing with the numeral. Same Evolution Chart line, just quieter.
4
Movement delta · signed, tinted
↑ 11 pts in a green-tint pill (--accent-live); ↓ would carry --accent-danger. Stated in points, not a vague "trending up." The “Yes” label sits under it so the number's meaning is never ambiguous.
5
The aggregation mark — our signature
A 3-dot cluster + “from 4 sources” states plainly that this number is a blend, not one book's quote. Quiet by default; “Details ›” expands the per-source breakdown. This line is the thing no rival market page can print.

Why a ladder, not eight cards

A “≥ N” market is really one continuous question. Stacking it as eight separate yes/no cards (the old feed behavior) hides the shape. The ladder restores it: every rung is cumulative, the heat reads top-down, and the distribution strip shows where the score actually lands.

Each rung carries a chevron — tap it and the history chart re-scopes to that single threshold. No rung is a buy button; it's a lens.

Heat encoding
Single-hue emerald ramp — lightness, not color, carries probability. Stays legible for color-vision deficiency and prints clean in the SwiftUI build.

The disagreement is the story

Kalshi shows only Kalshi; Polymarket only theirs. We're the one page that can show a 13-point split in a single frame — and when it's this wide, the block stops being a footnote and climbs to sit directly under the hero.

Still neutral: a number line with four dots and a blended marker. No source is colored "right," no spread is called a mistake. The reader decides what a split means.

Reflow, not redesign
Every component is the same one shipped to iPhone — the hero numeral, the Evolution Chart, the aggregation rows, the resolution panel. Desktop only changes the arrangement: the vertical stack splits into chart-main + aside, the aggregation opens by default (there's room), and the key facts that were inline on mobile lift into a rail. No web-only interactions, so SwiftUI keeps full parity.
