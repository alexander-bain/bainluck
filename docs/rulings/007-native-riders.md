## Why the consent gate is a rider and not a queue

It is small, it is not a feature, and it will never win a priority argument against visible
native work — so as its own queue it waits indefinitely. As a rider it ships with whatever native
work runs first. The classification matters: **App Store and privacy exposure**, not polish. A
polish item may slip; a compliance item attached to the next native session cannot slip without
that session noticing.

Swift in CodeQL is the same shape — one session, under the daily cap, riding rather than queuing.

## What the deferral actually says

Slot rotation waits on the calibration exit exam being green, which is consistent with the exam
ruling: **a queue that moves no exam item does not move the lane toward rotation.** This makes
the converse explicit too — no amount of readiness elsewhere rotates the slot early. The exam is
the trigger, and it is the only trigger.

Recorded here rather than left in conversation because a deferral that is not written down reads
as an open question, and gets re-asked. It is answered: not until the exam is green.
